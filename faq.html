General questions

About D1 Now
What is D1 Now?

D1 Now is a HRB funded research programme focused on improving care and support for young adults living with type 1 diabetes in Ireland. It brings together young adults, healthcare professionals, and researchers to design and test care that better fits real life.

Who is D1 Now for?

D1 Now is mainly focused on young adults aged 18 to 25 living with type 1 diabetes. It also supports diabetes clinics, healthcare professionals, researchers, and people interested in youth-centred health research.

Is D1 Now only for people in Ireland?

The current trial and clinic work are based in Ireland. Some resources, learning, and reusable materials may be useful to other teams working with young adults, type 1 diabetes, or patient and public involvement.

Is this medical advice?

No. This website gives information about D1 Now research and related resources. It does not replace advice from your diabetes team, GP, emergency services, or another healthcare professional.

Young Adult Panel questions

YAP

The Young Adult Panel (YAP) is a group of young adults with lived experience of type 1 diabetes who help shape D1 Now.

What is the YAP?

The YAP is a group of young adults living with type 1 diabetes who work with the research team. Members help shape study materials, website content, recruitment approaches, resources, and how D1 Now communicates with young adults.

Who can join the YAP?

The YAP is for young adults with lived experience of type 1 diabetes. Specific recruitment criteria may depend on the current stage of the project, but we aim to include a range of voices, backgrounds, experiences, and perspectives.

Do I need research experience to join?

No. You do not need research experience. Your lived experience and ideas are valuable. The team will explain the work clearly and support you to take part.

What would I do as a YAP member?

YAP members may review website pages, help shape recruitment materials, lead dissemination work, refine trial protocol, share views in co-design sessions, contribute to blogs or videos, and advise on what feels clear, relevant, and respectful for young adults.

How much time does it take?

The time commitment can vary. Activities may include short meetings, online feedback, or reviewing materials. The team will explain what is involved before each activity.

Will I be paid for YAP work?

Where YAP members contribute to approved project activities, payment or recognition arrangements will be explained in advance. The approach should be fair, transparent, and appropriate to the activity.

Can I join if I feel nervous about speaking in a group?

Yes. There are different ways to contribute. You may be able to share feedback in writing, speak in smaller groups, or take part in ways that feel more comfortable.

Will I have to share personal medical details?

No. You do not have to share anything you are not comfortable sharing. YAP discussions focus on improving research and care, not asking you to disclose private health information.

Trial participant questions

Joining the trial
What is the D1 Now trial?

The D1 Now trial is testing whether the D1 Now intervention can improve outcomes and experiences for young adults living with type 1 diabetes. The trial involves participating diabetes clinics in Ireland.

Who can take part in the trial?

Trial eligibility depends on the approved study criteria. In general, the study focuses on young adults aged 18 to 25 living with type 1 diabetes and attending participating diabetes services.

What happens if I register interest?

A member of the D1 Now team will contact you with more information. Registering interest does not mean you have agreed to take part. You can ask questions before deciding.

Do I have to take part if I ask for information?

No. Asking for information or registering interest does not commit you to taking part. You can decide after reading the information and speaking with the study team.

What would taking part involve?

The exact steps will be explained in the participant information materials. You may be asked to complete surveys, attend usual clinic appointments, provide study measurements, and allow the team to collect approved study information.

Will I definitely receive the D1 Now intervention?

Not necessarily. In a trial, participating clinics may be assigned to different groups. Some participants may receive standard care, while others may be in clinics delivering the D1 Now intervention. The team will explain this clearly before you decide.

Can I leave the study after joining?

Yes. Participation is voluntary. You can withdraw from the study. The participant information sheet will explain what happens to any data already collected.

Will taking part affect my usual diabetes care?

Taking part should not stop you from receiving your usual diabetes care. Your clinical care remains with your diabetes team.

Is there a payment for taking part?

No.

Can my parent, partner, or friend ask questions too?

You can speak with people you trust before deciding. However, the decision to take part is yours if you are eligible and able to consent.

Clinic and site questions

For healthcare teams
What is expected from participating clinics?

Participating clinics support recruitment, trial delivery, communication with eligible young adults, and collection of approved study information. Site-specific responsibilities will be explained through study training and site materials.

Who should clinic staff contact with trial questions?

Clinic staff should contact the D1 Now study team using the agreed trial communication route or the contact details provided in site materials.

Will clinics receive training?

Yes. Training and supporting materials will be provided to participating sites where needed. This may include information on recruitment, study procedures, intervention delivery, and documentation.

Are there clinic-facing resources?

Yes. Clinic-facing resources may include posters, quick guides, recruitment materials, site packs, and supporting documents. Some resources will be shared through the resources page as they become available.

How should clinics explain the study to young adults?

Clinics should use the approved participant-facing materials. The aim is to explain the study clearly without creating confusion or unrealistic expectations.

What if a young adult asks whether they will get the intervention?

Staff should explain that trial allocation depends on the study design and site allocation. The participant information materials should be used to explain this in a consistent and accurate way.

Privacy, consent and data

Your information
How will my information be used if I complete a contact form?

Your information will only be used to respond to your enquiry or contact you about the D1 Now activity you selected. The website should only collect the information needed for that purpose.

Will my data be handled securely?

D1 Now aims to handle personal information securely and in line with GDPR requirements. Full study data handling will be explained in official participant information and consent materials.

Does registering interest mean I have given trial consent?

No. Registering interest is not the same as giving consent to take part in the trial. Trial consent will involve official information materials and a separate consent process.

Where can I read the privacy policy?

Trial-specific privacy and data information will be included in the official study documents.
