About the programme

What is the D1 Now programme?

D1 Now is a randomised controlled trial (RCT) that is testing a new model of diabetes care for young adults aged 18–25 living with Type 1 Diabetes in Ireland. The model was developed with the Young Adult Panel (YAP), clinicians, and researchers, and is being delivered in routine diabetes clinics across 12 hospital sites.

The programme focuses on three core areas: building strong relationships between young adults and their diabetes team, making space for what matters in everyday life, and supporting shared decisions about care.

The trial will help us understand whether this new way of working improves engagement with clinic visits, confidence in managing diabetes, and overall well-being during a key life stage.

Why it matters

Why focus on young adulthood?

Young adulthood is a time of big life changes – moving out, college or work, new relationships, and shifting routines. For young people living with Type 1 Diabetes, these transitions can make it harder to stay engaged with clinic care and daily diabetes tasks. D1 Now aims to understand and respond to these realities.

The challenge

Staying connected to care

Many young adults miss or delay clinic appointments, feel judged about numbers, or struggle to fit diabetes into busy lives. This can affect health, confidence, and future outcomes.

Our response

A new model of support

The D1 Now intervention brings together a dedicated support person, tools to help young adults prepare for clinic visits, and ways of working that keep real life at the centre of care.

The impact

Informing future services

By evaluating this model in a multi-site trial, we hope to inform how diabetes services are organised and funded, and to strengthen youth-centred care across Ireland and beyond.

Co-design

Developed with the Young Adult Panel (YAP)

Young adults with Type 1 Diabetes are not just participants in D1 Now – they are partners. The Young Adult Panel (YAP) has shaped the intervention, study materials, and this website, ensuring the project reflects lived experience as well as clinical priorities.

How young adults are involved

  • Identifying what makes clinic visits helpful, stressful, or overwhelming.
  • Co-designing tools for clinic preparation and follow-up.
  • Advising on recruitment materials, language, and imagery.
  • Reviewing study documents and supporting trial roll-out.
  • Co-creating content for this website and future resources.
Our principle: The study should feel recognisable and relevant to the people it is for. That means clear language, realistic expectations, and space for young adults’ goals and concerns to guide how care is planned and delivered.

Governance

Ethical oversight and funding

D1 Now is being carried out in line with national and international standards for research ethics and data protection. The trial is sponsored and monitored through established academic and clinical governance structures.

Ethics and approvals
The study has received ethical approval from the relevant Research Ethics Committees and follows Good Clinical Practice guidance for non-CTIMP, multi-centre trials. Young adults receive clear information about what the study involves and can choose to take part or withdraw at any time, without affecting their routine care.

Data are handled in line with GDPR and institutional policies. Participation is voluntary, and all materials are written in plain language wherever possible.
Funding and partners
D1 Now is supported by national research funding, with institutional and clinical partners across Ireland.
